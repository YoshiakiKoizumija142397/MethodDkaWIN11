MethodDka Help

Overview

MethodDka is a program designed to perform factorization and solve higher-order algebraic equations. The high-precision version performs calculations with 50-digit precision.

Mathematical Background

Linear equations are learned in the first year of junior high school, and quadratic equations in the third year. Cubic and higher-order equations can be solved using Cardano’s formula or numerical methods such as the Lagrange method or Newton’s method. For fourth-order and higher equations, numerical analysis is essential except in special cases.

Many programs require initial values or can only find real solutions. MethodDka can find all complex roots of high-degree (up to 1000th degree) polynomials with real coefficients, without requiring any initial values.

How to Use

  1. Enter the highest degree.
  2. The coefficient input boxes will appear.
  3. Enter the real-number coefficients from the highest degree downward.
  4. If a degree does not exist, you must enter 0 (cannot be omitted).
  5. If a coefficient is +1, enter “1”; if -1, enter “-1”.

The significant digits of the solutions are approximately 50 digits.

Technical Background (About the DKA Method)

Typical factors that cause the Durand–Kerner–Aberth (DKA) method to fail or converge poorly:

  • Inappropriate initial value selection → If multiple roots exist, the denominator approaches zero and becomes unstable.
  • Presence of closely spaced roots → Strong interactions between roots slow convergence.
  • Extreme coefficient scaling → Initial radius estimation becomes inaccurate.
  • Insufficient iteration count or tolerance settings
  • Accumulation of rounding errors → Particularly significant in complex-number calculations.

Tips for Improvement

  • Using Aberth’s initial values greatly improves convergence.
  • For closely spaced roots, combining with methods like Jenkins–Traub is effective.
  • Scaling and normalizing coefficients improves stability.
